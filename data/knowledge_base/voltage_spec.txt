SKYROOT-M1 Electrical Power System — Bus Voltage Specification
Document ID: doc_001 | Tier: CRITICAL | Subsystem: EPS

The primary power bus for SKYROOT-M1 operates at a regulated nominal bus voltage of 28 to 32 V under all nominal mission phases including eclipse, sunlit, and safe-mode operations. The bus voltage regulator maintains output within this range to ensure all downstream loads receive power within their specified input tolerances. Any deviation outside the nominal bus voltage band must be investigated immediately to prevent cascading subsystem failures.

The nominal operating range for the EPS bus voltage is 27.5 V (lower warning threshold) to 32.5 V (upper warning threshold). Sustained bus voltage below 27.5 V indicates insufficient solar panel output or elevated battery discharge, and the flight computer shall initiate load-shedding protocols. Bus voltage sustained above 32.5 V indicates regulator saturation or shunt failure and requires immediate corrective action to protect sensitive avionics components.

The battery discharge operational range extends from 22.5 V to 27.5 V during eclipse periods when solar panels are not generating power. Battery voltage below 22.5 V triggers an autonomous safe-mode transition. The battery must be restored to a state of charge corresponding to a terminal voltage of at least 24.0 V before normal operations may resume. Repeated deep discharge events below 22.5 V will degrade battery capacity and reduce mission lifetime.

During peak power demand phases, such as active attitude maneuvers and high-rate downlink operations, the bus voltage may transiently drop to 27.0 V for periods not exceeding 5 seconds. If the bus voltage drops below 27.0 V for more than 5 seconds, the EPS controller shall automatically disconnect non-essential loads and generate a BUS_UNDERVOLTAGE anomaly report. The mission operations team must verify the load balance and solar array pointing before restoring shed loads.

The upper voltage limit of 33.0 V is defined as the absolute maximum rating for all bus-powered components. Exceedance of 33.0 V for any duration will result in permanent damage to voltage-sensitive electronics including the S-band transmitter and attitude control electronics. The overvoltage protection circuit must engage within 10 milliseconds of detection. Operate the bus within the nominal range of 28 to 32 V at all times during nominal mission phases to ensure maximum system reliability and longevity.

Solar array open-circuit voltage is nominally 38.5 V and must not exceed 42.0 V under any illumination condition. The maximum power point voltage is 34.2 V at the beginning of life, degrading to 31.8 V at end of life due to radiation damage. The shunt regulator dissipates excess solar power to maintain bus regulation; verify shunt temperature telemetry is within limits when solar array current exceeds 6 A. Investigate any condition where bus voltage is sustained outside the 27.5 to 32.5 V range for more than 30 seconds.
