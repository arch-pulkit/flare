SKYROOT-M1 Propulsion Subsystem — Thermal Specification
Document ID: doc_002 | Tier: CRITICAL | Subsystem: PROP/THERMAL

The propulsion subsystem thermal control system maintains thruster temperatures within the nominal operating range of 280 degC to 380 degC during all mission phases. The thruster catalyst bed must be preheated to a minimum temperature of 280 degC before any propellant valve opening command is issued. Failure to maintain minimum thruster temperature results in incomplete propellant decomposition, reduced specific impulse, and potential thruster head damage from thermal shock caused by cold propellant injection.

The upper operating limit of 380 degC for the thruster catalyst bed temperature must not be exceeded during firing operations. Sustained thruster temperature above 380 degC indicates excessive firing duration, blocked heat rejection pathways, or catalyst degradation. If thruster temperature reaches 390 degC, the propulsion controller shall autonomously close all propellant valves and generate a THRUSTER_OVERTEMP anomaly event. Investigate any sustained thruster temperature above 380 degC before resuming firing operations.

During standby phases between thruster firings, the catalyst bed temperature will cool toward the thermal soak temperature of the spacecraft, nominally 15 degC to 40 degC in orbit. The electric heater system maintains catalyst bed temperature above the preheat minimum of 280 degC using a thermostat-controlled resistance heater rated at 8 W nominal power consumption. The heater duty cycle must be monitored to verify catalyst bed thermal health; a duty cycle exceeding 80 percent indicates inadequate spacecraft insulation or heater degradation.

Thermal margins for the thruster assembly are defined as follows: the lower survival limit is 250 degC when the spacecraft is in safe mode with heaters powered from survival heater circuits. The upper survival limit is 420 degC and represents the threshold above which permanent catalyst damage occurs. Both limits are considered mission-critical failure boundaries. Operate the thruster thermal control system to maintain temperatures within the nominal operating range of 280 to 380 degC to prevent performance degradation and mission loss.

The thruster feed line and propellant valve body temperatures must remain above the propellant freezing point of 1.4 degC at all times. Propellant line temperatures below 5 degC trigger a freeze protection heater activation. The propellant tank temperature is maintained between 15 degC and 50 degC by the passive thermal design and supplemental heaters. Thermal anomalies in the propulsion subsystem require immediate investigation to verify structural integrity of propellant wetted surfaces and seals.

Reduce thruster firing duration and verify catalyst bed temperature is within the nominal range of 280 to 380 degC before resuming nominal operations after any thermal anomaly. Monitor heater power consumption and duty cycle to verify thermal control system health. Any thruster temperature reading outside the specified range of 280 to 380 degC must be reported and investigated before the next scheduled maneuver.
