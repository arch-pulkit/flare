SKYROOT-M1 Electrical Power System — Solar Array Current and Detailed EPS Specification
Document ID: doc_006 | Tier: CRITICAL | Subsystem: EPS

The solar array current for SKYROOT-M1 nominal operations ranges from 2.0 A to 8.0 A depending on the solar incidence angle, array degradation state, and eclipse fraction of the orbit. The telemetry channel SOLAR_CURRENT monitors the regulated array output current at 2 Hz. Solar current below 2.0 A during the sunlit portion of the orbit indicates a failed solar panel string, array shadowing, or an attitude anomaly resulting in off-pointing of the solar arrays. Solar current above 8.0 A during high-incidence-angle orbital phases exceeds the shunt regulator rating and must be addressed by adjusting array orientation.

The solar array is composed of four deployable panels with a total area of 1.2 square meters. At the beginning of life, the array generates 12.0 W at the maximum power point under standard test conditions (1367 W per square meter illumination, 28 degC cell temperature). The power degradation rate is 1.5 percent per year due to radiation-induced cell damage. At end of life (5 years), the array output power is reduced to 11.1 W. The EPS regulator maintains bus voltage at 30 V by operating the array at the maximum power point; SOLAR_CURRENT at nominal solar incidence angle of 0 degrees is 4.0 A at beginning of life and 3.7 A at end of life.

The shunt regulator dissipates excess solar power when the array would otherwise generate more current than the spacecraft load plus battery charging requires. The shunt regulator is rated for a maximum dissipation of 8 W, corresponding to a maximum shunted current of 0.27 A at 30 V bus voltage. Shunt temperature monitoring must be performed during high-incidence-angle passes to verify thermal margins. Elevated shunt temperature above 65 degC requires load addition or array repointing to reduce shunted power below the thermal limit.

Battery charging current from the solar array is limited to 0.5 C rate, corresponding to 1.2 A for the 2.4 Ah battery pack. Total spacecraft load including battery charging during sunlit phases is 4.0 A to 6.0 A under nominal operating conditions. During downlink operations, the additional transmitter load increases current demand by 0.27 A. During reaction wheel desaturation maneuvers, peak current demand may reach 7.0 A. Sustained SOLAR_CURRENT above 8.0 A indicates incorrect maximum power point tracking or shunt regulator failure. Investigate any solar current reading outside the nominal range of 2.0 to 8.0 A.

The EPS primary power bus uses a regulated architecture with battery and solar array connected through isolation diodes. The battery open-circuit voltage ranges from 24.8 V when fully charged to 22.0 V at end of discharge. Battery state of charge is estimated from the combination of battery terminal voltage and integrated current. A battery state of charge below 20 percent represents the minimum acceptable level for mission operations and requires immediate load shedding and charging prioritization. Nominal operations require battery state of charge above 40 percent at the start of each eclipse period.

Monitor the solar array current SOLAR_CURRENT channel and verify readings are within the nominal operating range of 2.0 to 8.0 A during sunlit orbital phases. Solar current below 2.0 A during sunlit periods requires immediate investigation of solar array health, attitude pointing, and potential panel deployment anomalies. Solar current above 8.0 A requires investigation of shunt regulator performance and thermal status. Operate the EPS within the defined nominal range to maintain power margin for all mission loads and battery health throughout the mission design lifetime.
