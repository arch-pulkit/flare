SKYROOT-M1 Communications Subsystem — S-Band Transmitter Frequency Specification
Document ID: doc_005 | Tier: CRITICAL | Subsystem: COMMS

The SKYROOT-M1 S-band downlink transmitter operates at a center frequency of 437.050 MHz with a frequency stability requirement of better than 50 kHz over all temperature and power operating conditions. The nominal transmit frequency range is 437.000 MHz to 437.100 MHz, corresponding to telemetry channel TX_FREQUENCY readings between 437000000 Hz and 437100000 Hz. Frequency deviation outside this range indicates thermal drift of the oscillator or phase-locked-loop failure and must be investigated to prevent interference with co-frequency users and loss of ground station lock.

The lower warning threshold for transmit frequency is 437000000 Hz (437.000 MHz). Frequency below this threshold indicates excessive cooling of the oscillator reference assembly or PLL out-of-lock condition. The upper warning threshold is 437100000 Hz (437.100 MHz). Frequency above the upper threshold indicates oscillator thermal drift from elevated transmitter operating temperature, PLL failure, or deliberate Doppler compensation malfunction. Any sustained transmit frequency outside the range of 437000000 to 437100000 Hz requires ground station notification and transmitter investigation.

The frequency accuracy budget allocates 10 kHz for oscillator aging drift over the mission lifetime, 15 kHz for temperature sensitivity across the operating temperature range of minus 10 degC to plus 60 degC, and 25 kHz for Doppler compensation algorithm tolerance. The total budget is 50 kHz, consistent with the stability requirement. The ground station demodulator acquisition bandwidth is 100 kHz; a frequency error exceeding 50 kHz from nominal risks loss of ground station lock during initial acquisition. Verify transmit frequency is within the nominal range of 437.000 to 437.100 MHz before scheduled downlink passes.

The S-band transmitter output power is 2 W nominal with a 1.5 W minimum acceptable level. Transmitter power below 1.5 W results in link margin degradation below 3 dB and risks data loss during high-elevation passes. The transmitter consumes 8 W DC input power at 2 W RF output; efficiency below 20 percent indicates transistor degradation. The transmitter temperature must remain below 55 degC during continuous transmission operations; thermal shutdown occurs at 65 degC. Monitor transmitter temperature and output power alongside frequency telemetry for comprehensive transmitter health assessment.

The uplink command receiver operates at 437.525 MHz with a bandwidth of 200 kHz. The uplink and downlink frequencies are separated by 475 kHz to prevent direct interference. Ground station Doppler pre-compensation is applied based on predicted spacecraft velocity; the residual Doppler frequency error after compensation is less than 5 kHz at maximum line-of-sight velocity. Investigate any transmit frequency reading outside the nominal range of 437000000 to 437100000 Hz, as this may indicate ground station tracking anomaly or on-board frequency reference failure.

Operate the S-band transmitter within the specified frequency range of 437.000 to 437.100 MHz to ensure compliance with the allocated frequency coordination. Frequency operation outside the coordinated band requires notification to the frequency coordination authority. Monitor the transmitter oscillator temperature as the primary leading indicator of frequency drift; oscillator temperatures above 45 degC correlate with transmit frequency approaching the upper limit. Reduce transmitter duty cycle if oscillator temperature exceeds 45 degC to maintain frequency within the nominal operating range of 437000000 to 437100000 Hz.
